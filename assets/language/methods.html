Learning methods that survive real life

Methods debate is noisy. What follows is a synthesis that matches long-term adult learners, exam passers, and immersion residents—not influencer "fluent in 90 days" claims.

Input vs output balance

Comprehensible input (reading + listening slightly above comfort) builds intuition. Output (speaking, writing) tests retrieval. Pure input without output plateaus socially; pure phrasebook output without input shatters at N3. Aim for 70% input / 30% output in year one, shifting toward more output once N4 stable.

SRS (spaced repetition)

Anki, Bunpro, WaniKani, or in-app flashcards work when cards are short, sentence-based, and reviewed daily. Kill leeches (cards missed repeatedly) by rewriting with a mnemonic or deleting if low frequency. Do not let reviews exceed 30 minutes or you will quit.

Immersion (without moving)

Change phone UI to Japanese. Follow one Japanese hobby channel. Cook from Japanese recipes. Label your house. Pair immersion with lookup tools (this app's kanji lookup, Yomiwa, etc.). Moving to Japan helps, but disciplined self-study abroad beats passive expat bubbles.

Shadowing and repetition

Repeat aloud after native audio, lagging by half a second. Fixes rhythm and pitch better than silent reading. 10 minutes daily beats weekly hour blocks.

Grammar study order

Follow a textbook spine; do not hop YouTube channels randomly. One series to N4 completion, then supplement. The Practice grammar game reinforces patterns already introduced—it is not a first exposure course. Exam level tags (N5–N1) are explained under Exams & benchmarks.

Accountability

Exam dates, conversation partners, tutoring, or public study logs. Track weak skills (listening, kanji production, keigo) separately from "hours studied."

What to avoid

  • Romaji-only beyond week 2.
  • Learning kanji without words (characters without compounds die fast).
  • Skipping listening because "reading is easier."
  • Collecting textbooks without finishing one.
