iPhone and iPad for Japanese study

Phones excel at micro-sessions (SRS, one grammar point, one news article). iPads excel at split-screen textbook PDF + dictionary, Apple Pencil kanji grids, and larger reading. Neither replaces speaking practice or a desk course book — they compress dead time and travel.

Dictionaries and instant lookup

  • Monokakido Dictionaries (bundled Japanese app) — Professional JMdict-class stack; worth the money if you read daily.
  • Shirabe Jisho / Japanese (Renzo) — Strong free/low-cost options; good clipboard and Safari lookup.
  • Yomiwa — Camera OCR for signs and menus; essential first week in Japan.
  • Takoboto — Android-first culture but useful cross-platform habits; clipboard helper.
  • Midori — Classic iOS learner dictionary; still fine for N5–N2 drilling.

On iPad, keep a dictionary in Slide Over while Safari or Books stays full screen.

SRS and kanji volume

  • WaniKani — Kanji + vocab SRS with mnemonics; paid; web + app sync.
  • AnkiMobile — Only if you already live in Anki desktop; build sentence cards yourself.
  • Kitsun — Community decks; alternative SRS UI.
  • Kanji Study — Writing drills and stroke practice; pairs with pencil or finger.
  • Kanshudo — Web-first but usable on iPad; structured kanji missions.

Grammar and structured courses

  • Bunpro — Grammar SRS tied to exam levels; best with a textbook spine, not alone.
  • Bunpo — Mobile grammar paths; lighter than Bunpro.
  • LingoDeer Japanese — Polished bite lessons; habit builder pre-N4.
  • NHK World Easy Japanese — Free official app + audio; trustworthy hyojungo.

Exam practice (reading / listening / grammar — not speaking)

Standardized exams do not test production out loud. Apps below drill recognition and speed, not keigo on the phone with a human.

  • Migii JLPT — Large mock question banks, level filters; popular for N3–N1 cram.
  • JLPT Test (several publishers on the App Store) — Compare ratings; avoid clone junk with ads only.
  • Dr. Moku (kana) — Quick hiragana/katakana bootstrap.
  • Practice games in japanophile-mcp — Offline exam-style drills when the sidecar is running (see Practice page).

Reading and listening

  • Satori Reader — Graded articles with built-in lookup; paid; excellent iPad reading.
  • Todai Easy Japanese / news reader apps — Short news with furigana toggles.
  • Audible / podcast apps — Shadow NHK slow news; download before flight.
  • Apple Books / Kindle — EPUBs exported from your Calibre library (see Materials → ebooks).

Writing and Apple Pencil

  • GoodNotes / Notability — Kanji repetition grids; import PDF workbook pages.
  • Mazec — Handwriting input for searching unknown kanji.

What to skip or use lightly

Duolingo — Fine for streak guilt; not an exam path. Generic “Learn Japanese” apps with no reviews — usually phrase spam. ChatGPT as textbook — good for explanations you verify elsewhere; bad for “is my keigo correct” without a tutor.

Device workflow

  1. Morning: 10 minutes SRS (WaniKani or Anki) on phone.
  2. Commute: one Satori or NHK article with lookup.
  3. Evening iPad: course PDF + dictionary + optional Bunpro reviews.
  4. Weekly: export progress; do not let five apps each claim to be your “main” course.
