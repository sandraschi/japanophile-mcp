Keigo (敬語): honorific, humble, and polite language

Keigo is not optional polish for advanced learners — it appears in convenience-store announcements, clinic reception, email to teachers, and any service interaction. Mistakes sound blunt or oddly self-praising, not "foreign cute." This tab matches what a solid textbook (e.g. Genki II, Japanese for Busy People III, dedicated keigo workbooks) covers in structure.

Three pillars

Teineigo (丁寧語) — polite desu/masu, polite adjectives, go- prefix on some nouns. Default for learners addressing strangers.

Sonkeigo (尊敬語) — elevates the person you talk about (often the customer, boss, teacher, guest). Special verbs and auxiliary go + stem + ni + naru/suru.

Kenjogo (謙譲語) — lowers the speaker (or in-group) when the action touches someone out-group. Special humble verbs and o/go + stem + suru/itashimasu.

Rule of thumb: elevate them, humble yourself. Never humble the customer or praise yourself with sonkeigo on your own actions.

Memorize these as sets; mixing plain with respectful subject is a common error (× 先生が食べる → ○ 先生が召し上がる).

お／ご + stem + になる / する

Sonkeigo pattern: お + verb stem + になる / なさる / ください.
Example: 資料をお読みになりましたか。 (Did you [hon.] read the materials?)

Kenjogo pattern: お/ご + stem + する / いたす / 申し上げる.
Example: 後ほどお電話いたします。 (I will humbly call you later.)

Noun honorific: お/ご + noun — お名前、ご家族、お忙しい. Do not put お on foreign loanwords or things that sound odd (× おコンビニ).

Teineigo in practice

Every verb in ます-form is teineigo. Adjectives: い-adj → いです; な-adj → です. Requests: 〜てください (neutral polite), 〜ていただけますか (softer, service). Avoid plain form with customers unless you are matching their register deliberately.

Uchi / soto (in-group vs out-group)

When speaking to outsiders, humble your company and honor the listener's side: 弊社の田中が参ります (Our Tanaka will come [humble]) vs 部長がおっしゃいました (The director said [respect]). Inside the company, plain among peers is normal; keigo reappears upward and outward.

Fixed service phrases (memorize)

  • いらっしゃいませ — welcome (shop)
  • かしこまりました — certainly (humble acknowledgment)
  • 少々お待ちください — please wait a moment
  • お疲れ様です — to colleagues after work (not to customers as primary greeting)
  • 失礼します — entering/leaving room, on phone hang-up
  • よろしくお願いいたします — formal request/introduction closers

Email and business openings

件名 with ご — ご確認のお願い. Opening: お世話になっております. Body: humble company + respectful reader. Closing: 何卒よろしくお願い申し上げます. Sign with 株式会社 + name without over-humble self-titles unless your company style requires.

Common learner mistakes

  • Using いただく on the wrong person (× 社長がいただきました).
  • Double honorific (× お召し上がりになられる) — pick one layer.
  • お/ご on everything — sounds childish or parody.
  • Plain verbs to 先生・お客様.
  • いらっしゃる for yourself (× 私がいらっしゃいます).

Study sequence

  1. Lock teineigo until automatic (です／ます only with strangers).
  2. Learn the special verb table above with example sentences.
  3. Drill お〜になる / お〜する transforms on verbs you already know.
  4. Shadow convenience-store and train announcement audio.
  5. N2 exam: fixed patterns + reading comprehension of keigo-heavy notices; production at N1 business level still needs live practice.
