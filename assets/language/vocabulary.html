Vocabulary and lexicon

Japanese vocabulary mixes native Yamato words, Sino-Japanese compounds (kango), loanwords (gairaigo), and hybrids. Size estimates for educated adults exceed 50,000 lemmas; N1 exam word lists are a fraction of that but test-relevant. Build depth in domains you actually live in (food, transit, work, hobbies).

Word classes

Nouns rarely pluralize. Counters (josuushi) are mandatory for counting objects (一本 ippon, 一枚 ichimai). Verbs and adjectives carry inflection; adverbs often derive from adjective stems or repeat onomatopoeia (ばらばら, どきどき).

Kango vs wago

Wago (native) feels conversational and appears in child speech and folklore. Kango (Sino-Japanese) packs meaning densely and dominates news, law, and academia (問題 mondai, 経済 keizai). Two-kanji compounds (jukugo) are learnable as units once you know constituent kanji.

Homophones (douon igigo 同音異義語)

Same sound, different word — a persistent bugbear for learners and sometimes for natives in noisy or context-poor speech. English has a handful that bite in writing or puns (bow as weapon, bend, or ribbon; bank as river edge or financial institution; bear animal vs carry). The inventory stays relatively small because spelling often disambiguates and the consonant/vowel system allows more distinct syllables.

Japanese packs far more homophony into the same mora strings: a small set of open syllables (plus ん and long vowels) must serve thousands of morphemes, and many kanji share a reading (on or kun). Written Japanese usually separates meanings with kanji (or kana for grammar); spoken Japanese often collapses them to identical pronunciation, so listening and recall without script hurt more than in English.

Everyday collisions: hashi — 橋 bridge, 箸 chopsticks, 端 edge; kouen — 公園 park, 講演 lecture, 後援 backing/support; ishi — 意思 intention, 医師 doctor, 維持 maintenance, 異議 objection. Single-mora piles are worse: ki can evoke 木 tree, 気 spirit/mood, 期 term, 記 record, 機 machine, and more in compounds.

Extreme cases: exam trivia lists run to dozens of distinct lemmas for one reading (classic textbook shock: many shou or kou compounds). Some pairs differ only in pitch accent in theory (橋 vs 箸 are often both hashi with accent variation by region); in Tokyo standard both are commonly HAshi vs haSHI, but learners cannot rely on accent alone nationwide. Long compounds can still clash: 合成 (gousei synthesis) vs context where ごうせい readings stack in other words — when in doubt, natives also reach for rephrasing.

How spoken Japanese handles ambiguity:

  • Situational context — eating → chopsticks; driving → bridge. Default until context fails.
  • Kanji disambiguation in speech — 「橋のはし」「箸のはし」, 「公園の方のこうえん」, or naming the character: 「はし、木へんに…」 (spelling by component).
  • Periphrasis — avoid the homophone: お箸, 橋を渡る, 講演会 instead of bare kouen alone.
  • Repair — どっち？どの意味？もう一度言ってください; repetition with a clarifying phrase.
  • Writing and subtitles — kanji, furigana, or rubi in manga/news; SMS may use kana mix or emoji for disambiguation.

Study tactic: learn homophone sets as meaning + kanji + one fixed phrase, not as bare audio. Exam listening loves minimal context — train on short dialogs where the wrong gloss sounds absurd once you notice the collision.

Loanwords

English loans are adapted to mora timing; meaning may narrow (マンション mansion = apartment block). German, French, and Portuguese leftovers appear in food and music. Do not assume English etymology equals Japanese sense.

Synonyms and register

Japanese loves near-synonyms with different register: 食べる vs 召し上がる; 見る vs ご覧になる. Thesaurus study pays off at N2+. Core tools: JJ+ dictionary, Kenkyusha, monolingual definitions once you can read them.

Collocations and chunks

Memorize multi-word units: 気を付ける, 時間がない, 〜てみる, 〜すぎる. Corpus frequency beats random word lists. Sentence mining from anime with transcripts, NHK Easy, or graded readers beats isolated Anki cards for retention.

Corpus in this app

With data/kanji.db installed, vocab search and exam vocab games pull large tables (JMdict-linked examples). Without it, games fall back to small baked lists—enough to try UI, not enough for exam prep. Restore the corpus from repo docs if your dashboard shows missing data.

Suggested daily volume

  • Beginner: 8–12 new items/day with sentences, review SRS queue to zero.
  • Intermediate: 15–25 new, plus weekly reading that recycles old items.
  • Advanced: shift toward monolingual definitions and domain corpora (medical, IT, law) as needed.
