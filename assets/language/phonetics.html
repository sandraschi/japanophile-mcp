Phonetics, mora, and pitch accent

Japanese is not tonal like Mandarin (syllable-fixed tones). It uses mora (timing units) and pitch accent (high-low patterns within words). Bad pitch rarely blocks comprehension for learners, but native speakers notice flat foreign accent immediately.

Mora inventory

Each mora is roughly one beat. Long vowels are two mora (おばあさん obaasan). Geminate consonants (small っ) hold one mora of silence before the stop (がっこう gakkou). Vowel devoicing is common (です desu often sounds des').

Five vowels

/a i u e o/ — closer to Spanish than English diphthongs. U is often rounded but compressed between voiceless consonants. English speakers: avoid adding offglides to English-style diphthongs on Japanese vowels.

Consonants worth drilling

  • Fu — bilabial fricative, not English f or h.
  • R — flap, between l and r.
  • Tsu — single affricate, not t + su.
  • N — moraic ん assimilates (before labials → m sound, etc.).

Pitch accent patterns

Standard Japanese words have one accent drop (some are heiban — flat after particle attachment rules). Example famous pair: hashi (bridge vs chopsticks) differs by pattern, not vowels. Dictionaries mark Tokyo accent; regional varieties differ (Kansai patterns are not identical).

Study tactics: shadow NHK news briefs; use pitch-aware apps; record yourself; do not rely on romaji stress marks (English stress ≠ Japanese pitch).

Listening vs speaking

Listening lags when study is reading-heavy. Schedule daily audio even on kanji-heavy weeks: textbook CDs, podcast for learners, drama clips with replay. Standardized exam listening sections punish inference and fast dialogue, not only vocabulary.

Phonetic writing

Learners may see IPA in linguistics texts; textbooks use kana. Avoid romaji-only listening drills after month one.
