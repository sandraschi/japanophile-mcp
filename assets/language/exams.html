Standardized exams and benchmarks

This tab defines acronyms used elsewhere in the app. Outside this page we often say exam level (N5–N1) or exam quiz instead of spelling out test names every time.

JLPT — Japanese-Language Proficiency Test

What it is: The most widely cited foreign-learner benchmark, run by the Japan Foundation and JEES. Levels N5 (easiest) through N1. Offered twice yearly in many countries (July and December sessions; check local schedule).

Format: Multiple-choice only — Language Knowledge (scripts, vocabulary, grammar, reading) and Listening. No speaking, no writing composition. You can hold N1 and still struggle with unscripted conversation if you never practiced output.

Scoring: Scaled scores per section; pass/fail by total and section minimums (varies by level). Wrong answers are not penalized beyond a missed point — answer everything.

Level bands (orientation, not legal word lists):

  • N5 — ~800 words; basic survival; kana + ~100 kanji.
  • N4 — ~1,500 words; daily topics; ~300 kanji.
  • N3 — "bridge" level; longer texts; workplace themes begin.
  • N2 — Many employers' baseline for office Japanese; news with effort.
  • N1 — Dense texts, nuance, keigo in reading; still not translator license alone.

Uses: Resume line, university admission (some programs), personal milestone. Limitations: No oral certification; skews toward reading/listening study; official lists do not match every textbook's order.

Official site: jlpt.jp — registration windows are strict; use mock tests in Practice before paying.

BJT — Business Japanese Proficiency Test

Pearson / Japan Kanji Aptitude Testing Foundation. Computer-based; scores 0–800 + subscores (listening, listening-reading, reading). Tests business scenarios: emails, meetings, keigo context. Still not free conversation, but closer to workplace than JLPT alone. Useful if JLPT N2 is done and you need a business credential.

JFT-Basic

Japan Foundation Test for Basic Japanese. Used in some work visa / specified skilled worker pathways as a Japanese proof (alongside country-specific rules). A2-ish band focus. Check immigration guidelines — requirements change; this app does not give legal advice.

Other benchmarks

  • STBJ / NAT-TEST / J-CAT — Alternative JLPT-like tests in some regions; less universal on resumes.
  • University placement tests — One-off exams for exchange programs.
  • Conversation / interview — Employer oral tests, tutor assessments; no single national standard.

Which exam when?

  • General learner milestone → JLPT N5 → N4 → N3 …
  • Office job in Japan → JLPT N2+ often listed; add BJT if HR asks.
  • Visa category with language requirement → JFT-Basic or JLPT per current rule sheet.
  • Academic research → Department may specify N1 or internal test; ask early.

Am I ready for the next level?

Self-check before registering:

  • Two timed mocks at target level ≥ ~70%?
  • Listening section without transcript at exam playback speed?
  • Kanji/vocab gaps fewer than 20% of mock wrong answers?

App Settings → Target exam level filters quiz and game difficulty (N5–N1). Progress counters track exam-style questions in Learn demo and Practice mocks — they do not register you for a real test.

Study tactics by section (JLPT-shaped)

Vocab/Grammar: Error log from past papers; Bunpro / textbook alignment.
Reading: Daily timed short texts; eliminate two wrong choices systematically.
Listening: No subtitles on final pass; practice note-taking for dialogue questions.
