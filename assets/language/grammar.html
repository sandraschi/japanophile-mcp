Japanese grammar (working map)

Japanese grammar rewards pattern recognition over rule lists. This page is a map; your textbook and the Practice grammar game supply spaced repetition. Read sections in any order once you know hiragana.

Sentence skeleton

Typical order: [Topic] wa [Time] ni [Place] de [Object] o [Verb] — not every slot appears every time. The verb carries tense and politeness. Adjectives and nouns before the verb use linking forms (na-adj + da/desu, i-adj inflection).

Particles (high-yield set)

  • は wa — topic; "as for X …"
  • が ga — subject focus, required with adjectives of desire/ability in many patterns
  • を o — direct object
  • に ni — time, destination, indirect target
  • で de — location of action, means/instrument
  • と to — and (closed list), with (person)
  • から kara / まで made — from / until
  • も mo — also, even
  • へ e — direction (written へ, often pronounced e)

Verb groups and forms

Verbs split roughly into:

  • Ichidan (ru-verbs) — stem ends in -iru/-eru with predictable conjugation.
  • Godan (u-verbs) — consonant stems; more surface allomorphy.
  • Irregular — する、来る、する-compounds.

Core forms to internalize early: dictionary, masu, te, ta, nai, potential, imperative, volitional. The te-form is the glue of requests, progressive, sequential actions, and many grammar patterns.

Adjectives

I-adjectives (e.g. 高い) inflect like verbs for tense/polarity. Na-adjectives (e.g. 静か) need な before nouns and だ/です when predicating. Both modify nouns; mistake them and particles still parse but sound wrong.

Politeness (see Keigo tab)

Plain vs desu/masu is only the start. Honorific (sonkeigo), humble (kenjogo), and fixed service phrases are a separate study track — use the Keigo (honorifics) tab for textbook-depth coverage. Exam reading includes keigo notices from roughly N2 upward.

Complex sentences

Relative clauses precede nouns (私が買った本). Reason (node/kara), contrast (ga/kedo), condition (to/tara/nara/ba) each have nuance maps in upper textbooks. Causative and passive appear in N3–N2 band; do not rush them before te-form is automatic.

Common learner traps

  • Overusing anata — often omitted or replaced with name + san.
  • Translating wa as "is" — it marks topic, not equality.
  • Ignoring aspect — ている is not only "progressive" but often resultative state.
  • Mixing plain and polite in one clause without intent.
